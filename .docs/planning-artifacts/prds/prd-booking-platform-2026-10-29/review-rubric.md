# PRD Quality Review — Saunalöyly Booking Platform

*Reviewed 2026-10-30: `prd.md` (draft, updated 2026-10-30) and `addendum.md`, against the PRD quality rubric. `.memlog.md` was read for decided and queued items. Citations: `P L123` is a prd.md line, `Add L45` an addendum.md line. Findings: 0 critical, 4 high, 14 medium, 11 low.*

## Overall verdict
This is a strong, unusually honest PRD. It states a testable thesis, records each decision with its date and the alternative given up, and gives every Core FR consequences that a story writer can turn into tests almost line by line. The risk is four holes that tests would not catch and that would surface late: an overlap rule that leaves the "never oversells" guarantee open, seeded Windows that will not follow the calendar when the examiner runs the app, Guest emails that the decided Administrator-only Outbox makes unreachable, and an asynchronous payment model that FR-36 brings in without saying so. Each can be fixed before architecture starts, and none of the fixes requires reversing a decided item.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions read as decisions, with dates and an owner. The delivery order is marked "(decided)" (P L27), the store-level guarantee "decided 2026-10-27" (P L268), and each If-time position carries its date (P L898–L904). Addendum B is the standout. Every research-based rule sits next to the "Alternative not taken" (Add L66–L81). For example, it shows why the lenient Whole-sauna rule fails at 20 Seats booked.

Trade-offs name what was given up. The strict Whole-sauna rule "can refuse a Private sauna while the Venue still has room in total" (P L493). The Core is "deliberately narrower than the teacher's suggested minimum", and the PRD gives the reason (P L908). Anyone objecting on either point finds the objection already stated. Most Open Questions are real, with options (Q1) or an owner and a deadline (Q2, Q3, Q6). Two things fall short: two consequential choices are made by leaving them out rather than by stating them, and a few callouts are in the wrong places.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The store-level guarantee after the Core is decided by omission (§4.3 P L268; FR-10 P L277; Add L17–L18) — In the Core, the data store refuses over-capacity writes "as the brief requires (decided 2026-10-27)". For If time, FR-10 hands "How the same guarantee is enforced" to architecture. Addendum A, however, says "the research found no store-level constraint", and the check moves into "one serialised transaction per Window" (Add L18). So from §4.9 on, the brief's requirement probably lapses, yet the PRD still calls it "the same guarantee". The examiner may check this commitment, so it is a product decision, not just an architecture detail. It touches the 2026-10-27 decision, which covers the Core only. *Fix:* state it. Either write "from §4.9 on, the guarantee rests on one serialised transaction per Window; store-level refusal covers the Core only" with a `[NOTE FOR PM]`, or require a stored Seat capacity per Window, kept current by the same transactions, under a CHECK constraint.
- **[medium]** The cut line is left open, although the data to draw it already exist (§10.1 P L906; Q6 P L948; §8.2 P L863–L865) — "No backcasting from 15 December has been run", and Q6 waits for sprint planning. The inputs are already in the PRD. Planning takes "7–8 of the first 12 days" (P L865), there are exams on 16 November and 8 December (P L863), and the brainstorm's work windows (Add L282, not confirmed) leave roughly three weeks to build the Core's 23 FRs and the 19 If-time FRs (FR-24 to FR-42). Without an expected cut line, the examiner cannot see what "Functionality and scope (20 %)" (P L855) is likely to cover. §10.3 also cannot say how many If-time epics are worth outlining. *Fix:* run the backcast before epics. Add one sentence to §10.1 naming the last If-time item the plan expects to reach, keeping the decided order.
- **[low]** Some Open Questions are not open, and most have no deadline (§12 P L949–L958) — Q12 is "out of scope for the fictional exam version", Q13 is only a pointer ("Heating cases I11 to I23. Vision."), and Q15 is parked until "it comes back". These are records, not questions. Q7, Q8, Q10 and Q11 have no owner and no "decide before". Q16 states no question; it is already queued. *Fix:* move Q12, Q13 and Q15 to §9 or §4.17. Give each remaining question an owner and a deadline, as Q2 and Q3 have.
- **[low]** One `[NOTE FOR PM]` marks a settled point, while three live tensions have none (FR-35 P L584) — The note explains why the 10-minute Hold already meets WCAG 2.2.1, so nothing is left to decide. The payment model (FR-36), the side effect of the Outbox rule (FR-8 and FR-17) and the guarantee after the Core (FR-10) carry no note. *Fix:* turn P L584 into a plain sentence, and add `[NOTE FOR PM]` at the other three.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is very little furniture. Five named protagonists exceed the rubric's four-persona flag, but they are scenario actors for six UJs, not persona profiles, and they are tagged as placeholders (P L55). Each UJ is realised by named FRs. The two Guest groups in §2 ("Regulars" and "First-timers", P L39) earn their place by driving FR-1's "Before you come" section. The NFRs are specific to this product. NFR-7 declines to promise performance instead of inventing a figure. NFR-9 cites the dark-pattern rules it implements, and NFR-10 quotes Lisa's own "the database tables cannot be fake" (P L804).

The Vision could not be moved into another booking PRD. It names the shared-rooms problem, bets on capacity rules that can be tested (P L33), and says plainly that its differentiators are unproven ("Neither is proven", P L35). The comparables and aesthetic references in the addendum feed concrete defaults rather than decoration: the 72-hour Cutoff for Whole-sauna bookings, the 90-minute Windows and the anti-references in §6. There are no findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The PRD has a thesis and says it out loud: "if the capacity rules are precise enough to test, one person can deliver a booking core that never oversells, and then grow it feature by feature without rewrites" (P L33). Priorities follow from the thesis, not from what is easy. The Core is the capacity guarantee plus the thinnest booking flow and Administrator view that exercise it (§10.1). The If-time order comes with reasons: HTTPS first because Manage links arrive with §4.8, and Changes before Whole-sauna bookings because Changes depend on Vouchers (P L898–L899, L908). The counter-metrics are specific and have teeth. SM-C1 refuses to trade test depth for feature count, and SM-C2 forbids gaining speed by weakening the atomic check (P L937–L938). The MVP solves a problem and adds an experience layer, and its scope logic matches.

Two things weaken the argument. The success metrics prove only the first half of the bet (finding below), and FR-36 already breaks the second half (high finding under Downstream usability). Separately, §1 counts "an administrator closes a sauna for repairs" as half of "the hard part" (P L33), but Blocks only arrive with If time 7, the last v1 item (P L904). That order is decided and is not questioned here. One clause in §1 saying that the Block half of the bet is proven only if If time 7 is reached would keep the thesis honest.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Half of the thesis is not measured (§1 P L33; SM-1 P L921–L924; SM-5 P L933) — The bet has two halves: a Core "that never oversells", and growth "feature by feature without rewrites". SM-1 measures the first. SM-5 checks only that If-time features "behave as specified", which a rewrite would also satisfy. Nothing measures the second half, and FR-36 shows that it can fail without anyone noticing. *Fix:* add a secondary SM, for example "each If-time item lands with the Core's capacity and race tests unchanged and only additive schema migrations". The commit history that DR-4 already requires can confirm it.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
For the Core, an engineer can write tests straight from the consequences. There are numeric capacity cases ("With 42 of 44 Seats booked, a request for 3 is rejected and nothing changes", P L291), distinct failure messages (P L306), a double-submit rule (P L236), and a test switch for every simulated failure (FR-7, FR-14, FR-36, FR-49). UX requirements are bounds, not adjectives: two screen sizes, 24×24 targets, and the whole flow by keyboard alone (P L415, L424–L426). The If-time rules are tied to catalogue IDs and to five invariants checked by a model-based test (FR-31). Almost none of the "handles X gracefully" kind of wording survives. "Intuitive" (P L807) has no check, but that is Q2 and already queued.

The verdict is adequate rather than strong because of holes the specified tests would not catch. The overlap rule under the oversell guarantee has a gap, the seed stops matching the calendar, one invariant contradicts its own examples, and several If-time cases have no rule at all: which Bookings can lose under D3, refunds of Voucher-paid Bookings, and edits to booked Windows.

### Findings
- **[high]** "Partly overlap" leaves the oversell guarantee open (§3 Window P L122; FR-13 P L295, L298; FR-38 P L622) — The Glossary and FR-13 forbid Windows that "partly overlap". FR-13's only test case is a pair of back-to-back Windows. Duplicate Windows (two 15:00–16:30 rows) and nested ones are not clearly partial overlaps. An example of nesting is a 15:00–18:00 Window, which FR-38 allows as "another length", around 15:00–16:30. Each Window has its own Seat capacity of 44 and its own counter in the data store (Add L17). Two overlapping Windows therefore sell the same rooms twice, while every FR-10 and FR-11 test still passes. This is the guarantee the thesis rests on (P L33). It touches the decided Venue-wide Windows (Add L251); the decision is sound, and only the wording leaves the gap. *Fix:* write "no two Windows overlap (half-open intervals)" in the Glossary, FR-13 and FR-38. Add one duplicate and one nested rejection case to FR-13.
- **[high]** Seeded Windows are not tied to the calendar (§4.4 P L314; FR-2 P L186; NFR-1 P L795; NFR-10 P L804; SM-4 P L930) — Windows are stored rows that "come from seed data", and the Core has no screen for adding them (P L314, L879). A date is offered only if it has a Window inside the 4-week Booking horizon (P L186). Nothing says the seed is generated relative to the day the app runs. A seed with fixed November dates works throughout the build. Then, when the examiner runs the app after 15 December, it shows an empty date picker, while SM-4 ("A fresh clone starts, and all its tests pass") still passes. The seeded sample Bookings in DR-2 (P L843) have the same problem. The rules that depend on time (Booking deadline, Cutoff, Release time, Hold expiry) also need a clock that tests can control, and no NFR asks for one. Q9 even ties Hold expiry to "the data store's clock" (P L951). *Fix:* add a consequence to §4.4 or NFR-1: "On start, Windows exist from the Window schedule for the whole Booking horizon from today, and the sample Bookings are in them." Add to NFR-6 that rules which depend on time are tested against a controlled clock.
- **[medium]** INV-5 contradicts its own examples (FR-31 P L527; P L509, L535, L655, L669) — INV-5 reads "no Sauna earlier in the Fill order has room while a later one holds Seats". A Whole-sauna-booked, Blocked or unreleased Whole-booking-only Sauna early in the order holds no Seats (INV-3, INV-4). Read literally, it therefore "has room" while later Saunas hold Seats. That fails B5 ("Seats are counted around the Sauna", P L509), D2 (P L655) and FR-41 (P L669). The model-based test uses INV-5 as its reference (P L535), so this wording will turn into code. *Fix:* "no Sauna that is open for Seats and earlier in the Fill order has room while a later one holds Seats", matching the Room ahead definition (P L137).
- **[medium]** "The nearest Windows" are promised but not defined (UJ-1 P L68; FR-3 P L196; FR-12 P L292; FR-22 P L417) — In UJ-1's edge case, a Guest who loses the last Seats at payment "is offered the nearest Windows with room for 2". FR-11 and FR-12 only state the message (P L284, L292), and FR-22 only says "with alternatives". FR-3's rule gives no count. Its tag "same day first" is also empty there, because the rule applies only "If no Window on the date has room". This is Core, and as written no story can test it. *Fix:* write one rule and reference it from FR-3, FR-11 and FR-12. For example: "up to three Windows with room for N, later on the same day first, then the following days within the Booking horizon".
- **[medium]** The Whole-sauna flow inherits the Seat flow only by implication (§4.9 P L489–L491; FR-5 P L222, L225; FR-30 P L519; FR-33 P L553) — FR-5 to FR-8 are written for Seats ("the number of Seats", P L222). Until §4.8, FR-5's terms state that "a Booking cannot be changed or refunded online" (P L225). A Whole-sauna booking, however, "can be cancelled for a refund ... until 72 hours before the start" (P L491). The PRD never says that the review, terms, pay button, double-submit rule (FR-6) and failure path (FR-7) apply with the Sauna in place of a Seat count. FR-30 adds only that the confirmation names the Sauna. Moving a booking to another Sauna (P L553) has no price rule, even though each Sauna has "its own price" (P L489). The queued price-model item does not cover this, because it is about Seat prices per Window. *Fix:* add to FR-29: "FR-5 to FR-9 and FR-14 apply, with the Sauna and its price in place of the Seat count, and the terms state the 72-hour Cutoff and the refund." Give FR-33 a rule for price differences, or allow moves only within the same Sauna.
- **[medium]** If-time pages have no layout or accessibility requirement (FR-22 P L412; FR-23 P L420; FR-51 P L777; SM-3 P L929) — FR-22 and FR-23 cover "every Core page". Several pages built in v1 fall outside them: Manage, Private sauna, Voucher entry, resend, guest account and the Administrator tools. They get FR-23 only through FR-51 ("FR-23 holds at every width"), the last Step 2 item. As written, the v1 blocking calendar does not have to work with the keyboard. *Fix:* apply FR-22 and FR-23 to every page as it is built. Have SM-3 report on the If-time pages that were built.
- **[medium]** D3 does not say which Bookings can lose (FR-40 P L656–L659; FR-42 P L679; FR-26 P L465) — The decided rule (memlog L27) is that the latest Bookings lose and are cancelled in full. Two cases are still open. First, it is unclear whether a Whole-sauna booking of a Sauna that is not Blocked can lose; cancelling one frees its whole Capacity. Second, "most recently created" (P L657) is unclear for Bookings changed under FR-26. A full move "keeps its reference". A partial move creates "a new Booking", which would then lose first even though the Guest booked early. This touches decided D3; the decision stands, and only which Bookings it covers is open. *Fix:* in FR-40, name which Bookings can lose (for example, Seat bookings only). State whether a moved or split Booking keeps its original creation time.
- **[medium]** Refunds of Bookings paid with a Voucher have no rule (FR-28 P L484–L485; FR-33 P L552; FR-36 P L591; FR-42 P L680) — Cancellations by Saunalöyly, Guest cancellations of Whole-sauna bookings and late payments all end in "a Simulated refund". Yet "A Voucher is never paid out in money" (P L485). If a Booking was paid partly or wholly by Voucher (P L484), a refund must either pay the Voucher out as money or restore it, and the restored Voucher's expiry is not stated. The decided choice between refund and Voucher (memlog L32) creates this case. Gift cards (FR-44) have the same gap. *Fix:* add one consequence to FR-28: "a refund returns the money part as money and the Voucher part to a Voucher, with a stated expiry".
- **[medium]** Editing or deleting a booked Window has no rule (FR-38 P L619–L626) — The Administrator can "create and edit Windows". FR-38 covers lowering Capacity (F1, refused), the Fill order (F2) and prices. It does not cover moving or deleting a Window that has Bookings, which would silently change the time Guests paid for. *Fix:* refuse to change the times of, or delete, a Window with active Bookings, or send the affected Guests through the FR-42 remedy. Add the case to FR-38.
- **[low]** Gradual opening contradicts Seats left (§3 P L132–L134; FR-50 P L766; FR-3 P L195, L198) — With Gradual opening, Seat capacity counts only Saunas that have been opened, yet "A Booking larger than the room left in the open Saunas opens the next Sauna". FR-3 would therefore show such a Window as unavailable and refuse the Booking. It would also show fewer Seats left than can really be booked, against "Any text about scarcity states the true current Seats left". This is Step 2, but it sits in the Core Glossary definition, which addendum A turns into "one capacity function from v1" (Add L23). *Fix:* before Step 2 is planned, define Seats left under Gradual opening as the capacity up to the Administrator's maximum, minus Seats booked and held. Use "opened" only for Placement and Door codes.
- **[low]** FR-42 says the fallback is used less than FR-40 makes it (FR-42 P L685; FR-40 P L656) — FR-42 says the fallback "exists for Blocks in Windows that have started, and for failures outside the platform's control". FR-40 applies the same rule (D3) to any Block that leaves too little Seat capacity, whether or not the Window has started. "Failures outside the platform's control" cannot be tested. *Fix:* write "it applies whenever Seats booked exceed Seat capacity, including after any Block (FR-40)". Drop the vague clause or name the failures.
- **[low]** A few bounds are deferred or are adjectives (FR-15 P L325; FR-18 P L355; FR-19 P L373; NFR-8 P L802) — "Repeated failed sign-ins are slowed or temporarily refused" waits for thresholds from architecture. FR-18's "a clear error" is bounded for forms (P L428) but not for API responses. "A list of common passwords" names no list. "Dependencies are current" cannot be tested. *Fix:* use NIST's cap of 100 failed attempts (Add L151) as the upper bound and define the API error shape. Name the password blocklist. Define "current" or drop it.
- **[low]** In the Core, the RB-4 test has nothing to target (SM-2 P L926; FR-8 P L259) — SM-2 runs RB-4 "for guessed Booking references" in the Core, where no page accepts a reference. The test can only aim at the confirmation page, and the PRD never says how that page is reached, for example on reload, without exposing the reference. *Fix:* add to FR-8: "The confirmation page is addressed by an unguessable id or the session, never by the Booking reference."
- **[low]** HSTS with a local certificate can follow the examiner beyond this app (FR-24 P L431, L440; SM-4 P L930) — Every HTTPS response carries Strict-Transport-Security, and a local development certificate is acceptable. A browser that stores HSTS for localhost will then force HTTPS on the examiner's other local services until the setting is cleared. *Fix:* in FR-24, require a short max-age for local runs or a dedicated development hostname, and state it in the README.

## Scope honesty — adequate
The apparatus is strong. §9 lists 13 specific Non-Goals, and all 41 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are indexed. De-scoping is recorded with dates and reasons (Add G). The PRD also commits that a criterion not reached "is reported as not delivered" (P L27). The number of open items is about 61: 16 Open Questions, 41 assumptions and 4 PM notes. That is high in absolute terms, but most are in If time and Step 2, and most are low-risk (text limits, 100 race repetitions). Only Q2 and Q3 block the next step, and both are already queued; Q4, Q5 and Q14 belong to architecture. The coach proposals (NFR-6, §8.2, DR-1, §10.3) are also already queued. So the open items do not block architecture.

The weakness is honesty about consequences: decided rules whose side effects the PRD does not spell out. The biggest one is the Outbox.

### Findings
- **[high]** The Administrator-only Outbox silently cuts Guests off from every email after the confirmation (FR-8 P L257; FR-17 P L347; UJ-4 P L91–L92; FR-26 P L465; FR-27 P L476; FR-34 P L566; FR-37 P L605; FR-42 P L680–L681) — "The Guest sees no other email" (P L257) is a decided item (Outbox 1A, memlog L25), and it closes a real leak. Its side effect is never stated: every later email lands where the Guest cannot look. Yet the UJs and FRs assume the Guest receives these emails:
  - In UJ-4, the Voucher "arrives by email", and "Later she uses the Voucher" (P L91–L92).
  - Under FR-26, a Booking split off by a partial move gets its Manage link "emailed at once".
  - FR-34's resend does nothing except send an email.
  - FR-37's "Sign-up does not reveal whether an email already has an account" assumes an email step the Guest cannot see.
  - FR-42's remedy link never reaches the Guest. In the demo, the 30-day default refund (P L681, already queued) therefore becomes the only outcome.

  Only DR-2 hints that the examiner fetches links from the Outbox (P L843). UX will draw "check your email" screens that lead nowhere, and stories will get acceptance criteria on the Guest's side that cannot pass. *Fix:* state one delivery rule in FR-8 and FR-17, and reflect it in UJ-4 and UJ-6. For example: the result page of a Guest's own action shows the email it sent, as FR-8 does; the Manage page lists the emails sent about that Booking, since the link already proves ownership; and FR-34's resend stays Outbox-only, labelled as demonstrable only through the Administrator.
- **[medium]** Codes that work for whoever holds them, other than the Manage link, have no security rules (FR-27 P L476; FR-28 P L485; FR-44 P L702; FR-42 P L680–L681; FR-21 P L403) — Voucher and Gift-card codes are money in the hands of "Whoever holds a Gift card code" (P L702). The remedy link triggers a refund choice and works for 30 days (P L681). FR-25's "at least 128 random bits. Only a hash of it is stored" (P L453) covers Manage links only. FR-21 keeps only "Passwords, Manage-link tokens and Door codes" out of logs (P L403). Nothing sets the length, guessing limits or storage for the other codes. FR-28's same-message rule (P L485) hides whether a code exists but does not stop guessing. *Fix:* apply FR-25's token rules to the remedy link. Give Voucher and Gift-card codes a minimum length of randomness, attempt limits as in FR-34, and FR-21's logging rule.
- **[low]** Several omissions are left for the reader to infer (FR-35 P L579; FR-15 P L326; FR-37; §9 P L869–L882) — An anonymous visitor can create any number of Holds, emptying a Window's Seats left for 10 minutes at a time. Cookie sessions have no CSRF or SameSite rule. The Administrator session has no expiry. Guest accounts have no password reset. The Administrator cannot make a Booking for a phone caller. Each may be right for the exam, but none is stated. *Fix:* add one consequence for each, or a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` line in §9.
- **[low]** FR-21's data claim stops being true as features land (FR-21 P L393; FR-34 P L567; FR-37 P L604; FR-43 P L693) — "For a Guest, the platform stores name and email only" stops being true with limits per source address (an IP address), password hashes for guest accounts, and Gift-card buyers. The privacy notice has to follow. *Fix:* phrase FR-21 per phase or list what each feature adds, and make updating the privacy notice part of each such feature's definition of done.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The PRD is mostly easy to extract from. It has a 36-term Glossary with a stated rule to use terms verbatim (P L25, L116), contiguous IDs, a test map from the brief's test IDs to FRs (Add K), an explicit planning depth for each phase (§10.3), and an information architecture that UX can sketch from (§7). Sections read on their own because they cite FR IDs and Glossary terms instead of "see above". Every UJ has a named protagonist with context. The catalogue (A1–I23) lives in the capacity research report. That is by design (P L19), but story writers will need that file open next to the PRD.

The problems are the ones architecture and the epics workflow will hit first. The PRD contradicts itself on the payment model, cites success criteria it never lists, has two Sauna marks with near-identical names, and gives Bookings statuses but no lifecycle.

### Findings
- **[high]** FR-36 brings in an asynchronous payment model that §4.2 and §10.3 do not account for (§4.2 P L212; FR-36 P L591–L593; §10.3 P L915; Q9 P L951) — In the Core, "Checking Seats left, recording the Simulated payment and creating the Booking happen in one atomic step" (P L212; Add L10). So a Guest who loses "is not charged" (P L74, L284). FR-36 then requires three things:
  - a refund for a late payer whose Seats are gone;
  - "if charged, a simulated refund" for the loser of a race;
  - that "Repeated or out-of-order payment confirmations never create two Bookings or two refunds", with a switch to "confirm late, twice or out of order".

  All of this can only happen if the charge comes before, and separately from, the capacity check: a charge-then-confirm model that removes duplicate confirmations by event ID (Add L39). §10.3 lists the If-time choices that shape the Core data model ("Placement, the lock unit per Window, Holds stored as data, and Manage-link tokens") and leaves this one out. Q9 treats it as something to prove, not as a choice. If the Core is built as §4.2 says, If time 5 will rewrite the payment path, which goes against the §1 bet on growth "without rewrites". It touches the decided move of Holds out of the Core (Add L244) and the brief's test CT-5. *Fix:* decide now, and add the outcome to §10.3 with a `[NOTE FOR PM]`. Option (a): payment stays synchronous. A late Pay re-checks and either books or refuses, nothing is charged on refusal, FR-36's refund and out-of-order consequences go, and CT-5's expected result becomes "not charged". Option (b): the charge-then-confirm model from the Core onward, stated in §4.2.
- **[medium]** Success criteria are cited 29 times but never listed, and SC7 is missing (§11 P L918; SM-1 to SM-6 P L921–L934; §10.1 P L895; FR-39 P L644) — The SMs are defined by reference ("This is SC1"; "This covers SC2, SC3, SC4 and SC8", P L933), and "Core done" relies on SC1, SC9 and SC10 (P L895). The PRD never says what SC2 to SC8 are. SC7 appears in neither file, so neither the examiner nor the epics workflow can tell whether it is delivered, dropped or Vision. *Fix:* add a ten-row table to §11 with the SC ID, one line of text, the phase, and the SM that covers it (or "dropped" with the date).
- **[medium]** "Bookable whole" is a second, undefined mark next to Whole-booking-only (§3 P L139; §4.9 P L489; FR-29 P L499; FR-38 P L619; FR-41 P L669) — The PRD has two Sauna marks with near-identical names. One is the standing "can be booked whole" set in FR-38. The other is the per-Window "Whole-booking-only" defined in the Glossary. Only the second is defined. FR-41 says a Sauna marked Whole-booking-only "can be booked whole" (P L669) without saying whether it must also be bookable whole. Architecture and UX could easily merge the two. *Fix:* add "Bookable whole" to the Glossary and state how the two relate, for example "Whole-booking-only applies only to a Sauna that is bookable whole".
- **[medium]** Bookings have statuses but no lifecycle (§3 P L126, L128; FR-16 P L339; FR-26 P L465; FR-42 P L683) — The Glossary gives a Booking "a status". FR-16 lists "active, changed or cancelled", with causes "Guest, Block or Overbooking fallback", and FR-42 adds the remedy states "pending, refunded or Voucher". Three cases are undefined:
  - the status of a Seat booking whose Seats are all given up for a Voucher, which is a Change, not a cancellation (P L128);
  - the status after a full move, which "keeps its reference" (P L465);
  - the status of the part split off by a partial move.

  Architecture needs the set of states, and stories need the transitions between them. *Fix:* add a small state table to §4.8 or the addendum, listing each state, the FR that enters it, the allowed next states and what the Administrator sees.
- **[low]** The remedy page is missing from the information architecture (§7 P L823–L828; FR-42 P L680) — §7's If-time Guest list has Manage booking, Voucher entry, Private sauna, resend and accounts, but not the remedy page reached from the apology email. UX may therefore not sketch it. *Fix:* add "Remedy page (from the apology email): choose a refund or a Voucher" to §7.

## Shape fit — strong
The shape fits the product and the stakes. This is a consumer booking site where the UX matters, so the six UJs with named protagonists carry weight. The Administrator is a single operator role and correctly gets only two UJs (UJ-3, UJ-6).
