1. Long Look

The place does the persuading. A wide image fills almost the whole first view, and the page then moves slowly through large pictures of the Saunas with a lot of empty space around them. A regular still finds Book seats, the hours and the price in one small panel, while everything else waits further down.

2. Timetable

This treats the website like the timetable on the wall of a public bath. Hours, price, every Window and the full Before you come text sit in the first view in small, tight type with thin rules, and the images stay small. Everything is answered at once, and the sense of the place comes from a short row of pictures.

3. Leaflet

This reads like the leaflet you are handed on a first visit. A calm reading column opens with what to expect and Before you come, with images in the margin, while a side rail with Book seats, hours and price stays in view as you scroll. The first-time Guest is spoken to first, and the regular never has to look for the button.

4. Four Rooms

The four Saunas stand side by side like signed doors along a corridor, under a bar where date and Seats can be set at once. Heavy, sign-like type and big numbers give it the firmest voice of the four, while the wording stays plain. A sentence directly under the rooms says that a booking gives Seats in the shared Saunas, not one room.
